        Choose Blink, then pick dates A and B. The view flips between them so differences are easier to see.

      A change on screen is a clue, not a discovery. Different wavelengths, image defects, and alignment can also make things look different.

      What do the other tools do?
      A/B residual
      Shows the difference between two dates after quality and wavelength checks. It is a rough comparison, not a precise measurement.
      Difference
      Compares each image with a combined view of the visits. Different wavelengths can leave misleading bright or dark patches.
      Scan for changes
      Finds possible changes in suitable images. Image defects can look like real objects, so every result needs checking.
      Spectrum
      Click a star, then choose Spectrum to see how bright it looks at different infrared wavelengths. Measurements are approximate.
      Motion trail
      Colours leftover light by date. A moving object may leave a coloured trail, but image defects can too.
      Deep stack
      Combines visits into one view. It can mix wavelengths, so use it to explore rather than make precise measurements.
      Where do these images come from?

      NASA/JPL-Caltech SPHEREx Quick Release images, served by NASA/IPAC IRSA. The six detectors cover infrared wavelengths from 0.75 to 5 µm. An object's brightness can look different simply because the wavelength changed between visits. These views do not provide calibrated photometry or confirmed detections.

      Name lookup: CDS Sesame. Orbits: JPL Horizons and IMCCE. Not an official NASA product. Source on GitHub.
